# Stakeholder Analysis

Stakeholders are grouped as **internal** (inside the organization building or funding the software) or **external** (outside it, using or affected by the system).

| Stakeholder | Type | Role | Interest/Expectations | Influence on the System | How the System Affects the Stakeholder |
|-------------|------|------|------------------------|--------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| Airport Operations Staff | External | End user; monitors overall airport activity and flight status via the dashboard | Wants an accurate, up-to-date, unified view of all flights, bays, and runways; expects the system to be reliable and easy to interpret at a glance | Their daily workflow and feedback directly shape UI/dashboard design and what data is prioritized on screen | Reduces manual tracking effort and coordination overhead, but a bug or unclear status display could lead to poor operational decisions |
| Ground Control Personnel | External | End user; manages runway assignment and aircraft movement coordination | Needs real-time, accurate runway availability and fast updates when assignments change; expects minimal delay between action and reflected status | Their operational rules directly influence the runway management logic the team must build | Speeds up runway coordination and reduces double-booking risk, but if the system lags or shows wrong data, it could cause real coordination errors |
| Airline Coordinators | External | End user; tracks their own airline's flights, bay assignments and status updates (read-only) | Wants to find a flight's status and bay quickly (NFR-10) and trust that the information is current; expects to see only their own airline's flights | Their information needs decide what the coordinator flight list shows, and the own-airline restriction (FR-06) | Lets them relay accurate status and bay information to their ground staff; a wrong or stale bay could send crews and passengers to the wrong place |
| Tower Status Feed Provider | External | External system that supplies automatic flight status updates (simulated in this project) | Expects a stable, well-defined interface for its messages | Its message format and availability shape the feed adapter (NFR-19) and the stale-data behaviour (NFR-08) | Little direct effect; FlightDeck depends on it, and FlightDeck keeps working without it (R6) |
| Development Team | Internal | Builds, tests, and maintains the system | Wants to deliver a stable, functional system that solves the coordination problem; expects clear requirements from stakeholders and reasonable timelines to build a quality product | Directly controls feature scope, architecture, and implementation quality; team decisions determine what gets built and how | They're responsible for ongoing maintenance, bug fixes, and future feature requests once the system is in use |
