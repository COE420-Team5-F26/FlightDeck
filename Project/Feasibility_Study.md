Exercise 5: Initial Feasibility Study
Conduct an initial feasibility study for your proposed project.
Your study should briefly evaluate the following:

A. Technical Feasibility
( Can the system be developed using the technologies, tools, hardware, software, and technical skills available to the team? )
At its core, FlightDeck relies on a standard full-stack architecture (frontend, backend, and relational database) utilizing frameworks and toolchains our team has practical familiarity with from prior academic projects. The primary technical hurdle beyond basic CRUD functionality involves maintaining data integrity for flight statuses, runway allocations, and bay availability under concurrent user operations—directly corresponding to the concurrency and state synchronization risks (R4 and R5) identified in our risk analysis. To mitigate this without requiring complex external infrastructure, we will implement established mitigation strategies such as optimistic concurrency control, update timestamps, and explicit collision alerts during the initial spiral iteration. Combined with our active GitHub repository structure established during Lab 1, the technical scope remains fully achievable given our existing skill sets and resources.

2. Economic Feasibility
( Are the expected costs and resources reasonable for the proposed project? Identify any expected costs, required resources, or limitations. )
From a financial standpoint, FlightDeck incurs virtually zero direct monetary expenditure. Our development pipeline relies entirely on open-source frameworks, free software tools, and basic tier hosting services for demonstration purposes, eliminating any need for software licensing or dedicated infrastructure budgets. The primary resource constraint is human capital—specifically, four team members distributing effort across multiple concurrent academic courses within a single semester timeframe. Consequently, time serves as our main limiting factor, reinforcing the necessity of strict scope boundary enforcement (R1) and well-structured task allocation (R2) to avoid overextending team capacity.

3. Operational Feasibility
( Is the proposed system expected to solve a real problem or meet a genuine user need? Are the intended users likely to use and accept the system? )
From an operational standpoint, FlightDeck directly addresses the real-world challenge of manual airport coordination, where managing flight updates, runway schedules, and parking bay allocations manually frequently results in human error and tracking difficulties when operational conditions shift—key concerns highlighted by our target users (airport operations personnel, ground controllers, and airline staff) in our stakeholder review. In a practical environment, user adoption would be high because the system significantly cuts down on manual oversight overhead, assuming the main operational dashboard remains stable, accurate, and simple to digest quickly as expected by users. Within the constraints of this academic project, operational success hinges on effectively addressing these central coordination challenges; our defined scope—focusing on live flight updates, runway and bay allocation, and a centralized monitoring view—is sufficiently focused to achieve this without requiring out-of-scope capabilities such as direct ATC links or automated conflict handling.

4. Schedule Feasibility
( Can the major project features and deliverables realistically be completed within the available course duration? )
Given the course timeline, we believe the project is achievable if we stick closely to the scaled down spiral approach outlined earlier. Since we're limiting ourselves to 2-3 cycles instead of a full commercial spiral process, each cycle can be mapped to a few weeks of the semester rather than dragging out indefinitely. The first cycle (real time data consistency) is the most technically demanding part of the project, so we plan to front load it early while we still have the most time buffer left before the deadline. The second cycle (runway/bay conflict logic) builds directly on that foundation, and the third cycle (dashboard and integration) is lower risk and can absorb any schedule slippage from the earlier two without threatening the core deliverables.
That said, we are a team of four with varying course loads outside this class, so the schedule isn't without risk. Tasks like writing the test plan, running tests, and putting together the final report tend to get pushed to the last minute in group projects, so we're planning to start documentation alongside development instead of leaving it for the end. As long as we keep to the cycle boundaries we've set and don't let scope creep (R1 in our risk table) eat into the timeline, we think the major deliverables are realistic to finish within the course duration.

5. Overall Feasibility
( Based on the above analysis, provide a brief conclusion stating whether the project appears feasible and identify any major constraints that may affect its successful completion. )

Taking the technical, economic, operational, and schedule factors together, we think FlightDeck is a feasible project for this course. The technology we're using is within what the team already knows or can reasonably pick up, the costs are minimal since we're not relying on paid infrastructure or licenses, and the problem itself is one that real airport staff genuinely deal with, so the operational need is there even in a simplified, course scoped version.
The main constraints that could affect successful completion are the ones we've already flagged in our risk table, mainly uneven work distribution and the concurrency/conflict handling logic being harder to implement correctly than it looks on paper. Keeping the feature set disciplined and not drifting into out of scope territory (like ATC integration or automated conflict resolution) will also be important for staying on track. With those risks actively managed rather than ignored, we're confident the project is achievable within the timeframe and resources we have as a team.
